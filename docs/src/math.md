# Mathematical Background

## Partial flag varieties

A **partial flag variety** is a homogeneous space ``G/P`` where:
- ``G`` is a semisimple algebraic group over ``\mathbb{C}``
- ``P \supseteq B`` is a **parabolic subgroup** containing the Borel subgroup ``B``

The parabolic subgroup ``P`` is determined by a subset ``I \subseteq \{1, \ldots, r\}``
of the nodes of the Dynkin diagram of ``G``. The convention in this package is:

> **Marked nodes = nonparabolic nodes = crossed-out nodes**

The parabolic ``P_I`` is generated by ``B`` and the negative simple root subgroups
``U_{-\alpha_j}`` for ``j \notin I``.

## The Levi decomposition

The parabolic subgroup ``P = L \ltimes U`` has a **Levi decomposition** where:
- ``L`` is the **Levi factor** (reductive)
- ``U`` is the **unipotent radical**

The Levi factor itself decomposes as ``L = Z(L)^\circ \cdot [L, L]`` where:
- ``Z(L)^\circ`` is the connected center (a torus of rank ``|I|``)
- ``[L, L]`` is the **semisimple part** whose Dynkin diagram is the sub-diagram
  on the unmarked nodes

## Equivariant bundles

An **equivariant vector bundle** on ``G/P`` is equivalent to a representation
of ``P``. Since the unipotent radical ``U`` acts trivially on the
**semisimplification**, equivariant bundles on ``G/P`` (up to filtration)
correspond to representations of the Levi factor ``L``.

An **irreducible** representation of ``L`` decomposes as:
``V = \chi \otimes W``
where ``\chi`` is a character of the center ``Z(L)^\circ`` (the "central part")
and ``W`` is an irreducible representation of ``[L, L]`` (the "semisimple part").

This decomposition is effected by the **decomposition matrix** ``M`` which performs
the change of basis from the fundamental weight coordinates of ``G`` to the
(center + semisimple) coordinate system.

## The Borel–Weil–Bott theorem

For an equivariant line bundle ``\mathcal{L}_\lambda`` on ``G/B`` (the full
flag variety), the **Borel–Weil–Bott theorem** states:

Let ``\mu = \lambda + \rho`` where ``\rho`` is the Weyl vector.

- If ``\mu`` is **singular** (lies on a wall of the Weyl chambers), then
  ``H^i(G/B, \mathcal{L}_\lambda) = 0`` for all ``i``.

- If ``\mu`` is **regular**, there exists a unique ``w \in W`` such that
  ``w(\mu)`` is dominant. Then:
  - ``H^{\ell(w)}(G/B, \mathcal{L}_\lambda) \cong V_{w(\mu) - \rho}`` as ``G``-modules
  - ``H^i(G/B, \mathcal{L}_\lambda) = 0`` for ``i \neq \ell(w)``

For a bundle on ``G/P``, we apply this to each irreducible Levi summand by
converting back to an ambient weight via the special matrix.

## Topological invariants

- **Dimension**: ``\dim(G/P) = |\Phi^+_G| - |\Phi^+_L|`` (positive roots of ``G``
  minus positive roots of the Levi)
- **Euler characteristic**: ``\chi(G/P) = |W_G| / |W_L|``
- **Betti numbers**: computed from the ratio of Poincaré polynomials of ``W_G``
  and ``W_L``
- **Picard rank**: equals ``|I|``, the number of marked nodes
